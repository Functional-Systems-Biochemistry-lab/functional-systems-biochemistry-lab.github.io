## Background

Most omics methods homogenize a sample and lose information about where molecules are located. Imaging mass spectrometry keeps that spatial information. A thin tissue section is coated with a matrix, a laser is fired at a grid of positions, and each position yields a mass spectrum. The result is an image for every detected mass, showing how lipids, peptides, or metabolites are distributed across the tissue without labels or antibodies.

## Approach

Sections are prepared, coated with a MALDI matrix, and measured on a MALDI-TOF instrument over a defined grid. Ion images are built from the spectra and compared with histological staining of the same or an adjacent section to link molecular distributions to tissue structures.
