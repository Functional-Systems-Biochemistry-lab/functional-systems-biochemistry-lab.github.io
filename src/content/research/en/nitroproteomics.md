## Background

Reactive nitrogen species form when nitric oxide reacts with superoxide, and they can modify proteins. A well-known example is the nitration of tyrosine residues. Such modifications accumulate under oxidative and nitrosative stress and have been linked to inflammation, neurodegeneration, and cardiovascular disease. Nitroproteomics asks which proteins are modified, at which sites, and whether the modification changes protein function.

## Approach

Nitrated proteins or peptides are enriched, for example with antibodies or chemical derivatization, and analyzed by LC-MS/MS. The characteristic mass shift of the nitro group allows the modified residue to be assigned from the fragmentation spectrum.
