## Background

A biomarker is a molecule whose presence or amount reports on a biological state, such as the onset or progression of a disease. Proteins are attractive biomarkers because they carry out most cellular functions and their levels change with disease. Mass spectrometry can measure thousands of proteins in one sample, which makes it a powerful tool for discovering candidates. The main challenge is to separate true disease signals from biological and technical variation, and then to confirm candidates in independent samples.

## Approach

We compare the proteomes of disease and control samples with label-free or isotope-labeled quantification. Candidate proteins are ranked by statistics and biological context, and then verified with targeted assays such as parallel reaction monitoring (PRM) or multiple reaction monitoring (MRM).
